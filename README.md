# Recations
Simon Says esque competition game.

I misspelled "Reactions" But its fine nobodys perfect and im lazy so im not changing it.

The project started as a complete mystery to me. I went into this week expecting some sort of guide, similarly to week 3. I really thought week 3's tutorial great since it did give instructions but I still didn't follow it fully. I made my own design, and I was going into this project with a similar mindset.
However, this could not happen. There was a large selection of displays, making it really hard to think of what to do. It's like Netflix Syndrome, where you have million different choices, but still can't decide. I get that a lot.
However, when I was reading through some of the things, I saw something: Simon Says. I saw it as a suggestion for a project, and it was like an LED lit over my head. The idea of competitive Simon Says came to me. It's not exactly Simon says, but it takes the classic lighting buttons design into it. I would have 2 button "Hubs," all connected. When the game starts, the lights would shine randomly on each. When you press it, it moves to another light. Its more reaction than anything, but it still works. 
Anyways, onto actually making it. I was able to apply my skills from the past 2 weeks in order to do this myself. I knew I needed a display along with the buttons. Enter the TFT screen. I still don't know if it's actually gonna work, but if it doesn't I can always just use my laptop to display the things from the terminal/codespace. The schematic was fairly simple, but wiring the lights for the actual buttons were kinda hell. It was only a simple parallel circuit, but figuring out the resistor values necessary and figuring out how to wire the buttons up (I'm still shaky on if it's gonna work or not, god I hope so). 
The PCB actually wasn't too bad though. What took me 4 hours last week only took about 1 or 2 this week. I got a pretty good PCB wiring going and things went pretty smoothly. I think I'm picking this up kinda fast, which is awesome.
However, real hell started when Onshape was opened. I know  I can't just use the basic push switch, that doesn't look like Simon Says at all!
So then, I opened it up. I made the base pretty easily, which was great. However, i don't know how to make a button at all. I sat for 30 minutes messing around with circles, before I finished. I got an exactly right measurement and decided I should do something with springs to make the button work, narrowing my margin of error to extremely low levels, about 0.2mm, which is INSANE for someone who just sucks at CAD. Then, after I got that down perfectly, I realized I made the little button segments on the wrong side. THIS was the real hell. I spent another 15 minutes just trying to fix this, until I realized that I could just rotate it.
